## 1. Overview

Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) was introduced by the **Companies, Partnerships and Groups (Accounts and Reports) Regulations 2018**, which amended the Companies Act 2006. SECR requires large UK companies and large limited liability partnerships (LLPs) to disclose energy use and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in their annual reports.

SECR applies to financial years beginning **on or after 1 April 2019**. Reporting must cover the same period as the company's financial year.

---

## 2. Who Is in Scope?

SECR applies to two categories of organisation:

### 2.1 Quoted Companies

All **quoted companies** are in scope for SECR reporting requirements, regardless of size. A quoted company is one whose equity shares are officially listed on the UK Official List, or admitted to dealing on the New York Stock Exchange or NASDAQ.

There is no minimum size threshold for quoted companies — every quoted company must comply.

### 2.2 Large Unquoted Companies and Large LLPs

Unquoted companies incorporated in the UK and large LLPs are in scope if they qualify as **large** under the Companies Act 2006 thresholds. A company or LLP is large if it meets **at least two of the following three criteria** in the relevant financial year:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Annual turnover | **£36 million or more** |
| Balance sheet total | **£18 million or more** |
| Number of employees | **250 or more** |

**Two of the three criteria must be satisfied** for the organisation to be in scope. Meeting only one criterion (for example, having 250 or more employees but turnover below £36 million and balance sheet below £18 million) does not bring an organisation into scope.

Note: **500 employees is not a SECR threshold.** The employee threshold for SECR is 250 or more employees, and it is one of three criteria — not a standalone trigger. A company with 500 employees but turnover below £36 million and balance sheet below £18 million would not qualify as large and would therefore be out of scope.

---

## 3. Exemption: Low-Energy Users

Organisations that are otherwise in scope are **exempt from SECR** if their total energy consumption in the reporting period is **40 MWh or less**. Such organisations must still state in their Directors' Report that they qualify for the low-energy exemption.

---

## 4. What Must Be Included in an SECR Report?

SECR disclosures must appear in the **Directors' Report** in the company's annual accounts. There is no standalone SECR report — the information must be embedded within the Directors' Report.

### 4.1 Required Disclosures for Large Unquoted Companies and Large LLPs

Large unquoted companies and large LLPs must disclose:

1. **UK energy use** — total annual energy consumption in kilowatt hours (kWh), covering at a minimum: gas, electricity, and transport fuel used in the UK (including the UK offshore area).
2. **UK GHG emissions** — Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions arising from UK energy use, expressed in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e). Scope 1 covers direct emissions from combustion of fuel; Scope 2 covers indirect emissions from purchased electricity.
3. **At least one intensity metric** — a ratio expressing emissions or energy use relative to a business-relevant denominator (e.g. tCO₂e per £m turnover, kWh per employee, or kWh per m² of floor space). The choice of metric is at the organisation's discretion but must be disclosed consistently.
4. **Energy efficiency actions** — a description of the principal energy efficiency actions taken during the reporting period.
5. **Methodology statement** — a brief description of the methodology used to calculate emissions and energy figures.
6. **Prior year comparison** — a comparison of the current year's energy and emissions figures with the previous year's figures (not required in the first year of reporting).

### 4.2 Additional Requirements for Quoted Companies

Quoted companies must report on a **global** basis (not just UK), and must additionally disclose:

1. **Annual GHG emissions from all activities** globally for which the company is responsible — including Scope 1 (combustion of fuel and operation of any facility) and Scope 2 (purchased electricity, heat, steam, or cooling).
2. **At least one intensity metric** — as for unquoted companies above.
3. **Energy efficiency actions taken** during the reporting period.
4. **Methodology statement**.
5. **Prior year comparison**.

### 4.3 Common Requirements Applying to Both Quoted and Unquoted Companies

The following requirements apply to all in-scope organisations:

- Disclosures must appear in the **Directors' Report** (or equivalent section of the annual accounts for LLPs).
- The **reporting period must align with the financial year** of the organisation.
- Where Scope 3 emissions are disclosed voluntarily, they should be clearly labelled as voluntary and not conflated with mandatory Scope 1 and Scope 2 figures.
- Organisations must use a **recognised GHG reporting methodology**, such as the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard, the ISO 14064-1 standard, or the BEIS/Defra emissions factors.
- Comparative figures from the **previous year** must be restated if the methodology or organisational boundary changes significantly.

---

## 5. Scope 1 and Scope 2 Definitions

- **Scope 1 (direct emissions):** Emissions from sources owned or controlled by the organisation — including combustion of natural gas, diesel, petrol, and other fuels in company-owned or controlled equipment, vehicles, and facilities.
- **Scope 2 (indirect emissions from energy):** Emissions associated with the generation of electricity, heat, steam, or cooling purchased and consumed by the organisation. Under SECR, Scope 2 emissions are calculated on a **location-based** basis using BEIS/Defra grid average emission factors, unless the organisation discloses both location-based and market-based figures.

---

## 6. Intensity Metrics

The choice of intensity metric is left to the organisation. Commonly used metrics include:

- **tCO₂e per £m turnover** (for commercial organisations)
- **kWh per employee**
- **kWh per m² of floor area** (for property-intensive businesses)
- **tCO₂e per unit of output** (for manufacturing)

The metric must be consistent and appropriate to the organisation's business model. The same intensity metric must be used for the prior year comparison.

---

## 7. Reporting Basis: 2018 Regulations

The legal basis for SECR is the **Companies, Partnerships and Groups (Accounts and Reports) Regulations 2018** (SI 2018/860), which amended:

- The Companies Act 2006 (for companies)
- The Limited Liability Partnerships (Accounts and Audit) (Application of Companies Act 2006) Regulations 2008 (for LLPs)

The 2018 Regulations replaced the previously mandatory Carbon Reduction Commitment (CRC) Energy Efficiency Scheme with the SECR framework, broadening the scope of mandatory carbon reporting while reducing the administrative burden of the CRC scheme.
