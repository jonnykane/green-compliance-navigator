## 1. What are the UK Sustainability Disclosure Standards?

The **UK Sustainability Disclosure Standards (UK SDS)** are a set of sustainability reporting standards being developed for use by UK companies. They are based on the **IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards** issued by the **International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)** in June 2023:

- **IFRS S1** — General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information
- **IFRS S2** — Climate-related Disclosures

The UK government has committed to developing UK SDS by **endorsing** the ISSB standards, with UK-specific modifications where necessary to reflect UK legal and regulatory context.

UK SDS are distinct from the **EU's European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)** under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). UK companies with reporting obligations under both frameworks will need to manage differences between the two.

---

## 2. What do IFRS S1 and S2 Require?

### IFRS S1 — General Requirements

IFRS S1 requires entities to disclose material information about **all sustainability-related risks and opportunities** that could reasonably be expected to affect the entity's cash flows, access to finance, or cost of capital over the short, medium, or long term.

Key requirements:
- Disclose across four pillars aligned with TCFD: Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, Metrics and Targets
- Apply **enterprise value** perspective (how sustainability matters affect the reporting entity financially)
- Disclose alongside financial statements (same reporting package)
- Use the same reporting period as financial statements

### IFRS S2 — Climate-related Disclosures

IFRS S2 builds on IFRS S1 and specifically requires climate-related disclosures:

- **Physical risks**: acute (e.g. extreme weather events) and chronic (e.g. sea level rise, temperature change)
- **Transition risks**: policy and legal, technology, market, and reputational risks from the transition to a lower-carbon economy
- **Climate-related opportunities**
- **Scenario analysis**: assessment of climate resilience using climate scenarios, including a scenario consistent with global average temperature increases of **1.5°C**
- **GHG emissions**: Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 (with a transition relief on Scope 3 in the first year of application)
- **Carbon credits**: disclosure of any use of carbon credits in climate-related targets

## 3. UK Endorsement Process

The UK government is following a formal **endorsement process** to adopt the ISSB standards as UK SDS. The process involves:

1. **UK Endorsement Board (UKEB)** — technical assessment of IFRS S1 and S2 for UK endorsement, including assessment of whether any UK-specific modifications are needed
2. **Government consultation** — public consultation on the endorsed standards and proposed mandatory application
3. **Legislation or regulatory rulemaking** — to mandate UK SDS for applicable UK companies
4. **FCA rule changes** — for listed companies, the FCA will update its disclosure rules to replace or extend the current TCFD-aligned requirements

## 5. Who is Likely to be In Scope?

The UK government has indicated that mandatory UK SDS will initially apply to:

- **UK premium and standard listed companies** (via FCA listing rule changes) — likely first wave
- **Large UK companies** meeting a size threshold (details under consultation) — likely second wave
- **Large private UK companies** — potentially in a later phase

The thresholds for unlisted companies have not been confirmed. The Companies Act 2006 definition of "large company" (two of three: >250 employees, >£36 million turnover, >£18 million balance sheet total) has been referenced as a likely basis, but the government has indicated a higher threshold may apply initially.

---

## 6. Relationship to TCFD and Existing FCA Rules

The FCA has confirmed that once UK SDS are mandated for listed companies, its current **TCFD-aligned listing rules** (LR 9.8.6R and LR 14.3.27R) will be **updated or replaced** by UK SDS requirements. TCFD-compliant companies will have a strong foundation for UK SDS compliance — IFRS S2 is explicitly built on TCFD recommendations — but UK SDS will require:

- More granular GHG emissions disclosure (including Scope 3)
- Formal climate scenario analysis using a 1.5°C scenario
- Cross-industry metric disclosures (transition plans, physical risk metrics)
- Integration with financial statements rather than separate reporting

---

## 7. Relationship to CSRD

UK SDS (ISSB-based) and the EU's ESRS (CSRD-based) are separate frameworks with different:
- Materiality approaches (UK SDS: enterprise value / single materiality; ESRS: double materiality)
- Disclosure scope (ESRS covers broader ESG including biodiversity, workers in the value chain)
- Legal obligation basis (UK SDS via Companies Act / FCA rules; CSRD via EU Directive)

UK companies with EU subsidiaries in CSRD scope, and who are also in scope for UK SDS, will need to produce disclosures under both frameworks. Interoperability between IFRS S1/S2 and ESRS has been assessed by EFRAG and the ISSB — there is significant but not complete overlap.

---

## 8. What Should UK Businesses Do Now?

1. **Build on TCFD compliance**: Companies already making FCA-mandated TCFD disclosures should use those as the foundation for future UK SDS compliance
2. **Assess CSRD exposure**: Determine whether EU subsidiaries trigger CSRD obligations before UK SDS is mandatory
3. **Develop Scope 3 capability**: IFRS S2 requires Scope 3 disclosure; data collection for Scope 3 is the most common gap and requires supply chain engagement
4. **Monitor government and FCA announcements**: The endorsement and consultation timeline will determine the mandatory start date
