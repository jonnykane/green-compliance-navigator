# FCA Mandatory Climate-Related Financial Disclosures: Summary of Rules (PS21/24)

**Based on:** FCA Policy Statement PS21/24 (December 2021); FCA Handbook (LR 9.8, DTR 7.2, COLL, COBS, SUP); FCA Consultation Paper CP23/20 (proposed enhancements)  
**Accuracy note:** Obligations, thresholds, and timelines reflect PS21/24 and subsequent FCA guidance as of mid-2024.  
**Last reviewed:** June 2024

## 1. Overview

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) introduced mandatory climate-related financial disclosure requirements in its Policy Statement **PS21/24**, published December 2021. The rules require certain FCA-regulated entities to disclose climate-related financial information consistent with the recommendations of the **Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)**.

The requirements build on TCFD's four-pillar framework: **Governance, Strategy, Risk Management,** and **Metrics and Targets**.

---

## 2. Who is In Scope?

FCA mandatory TCFD-aligned disclosures apply to the following categories of regulated entities:

### 2.1 Listed Companies (Listing Rules)

| Entity type | Rule | Applies from |
|-------------|------|--------------|
| UK **premium listed** commercial companies | LR 9.8.6R | Financial years beginning **on or after 1 January 2021** |
| UK **standard listed** companies (equity shares, excl. investment entities and shell companies) | LR 14.3.27R | Financial years beginning **on or after 1 January 2022** |

Listed companies must make TCFD-aligned disclosures in their annual financial report. Where they do not comply with all recommended disclosures, they must explain why (comply-or-explain basis).

### 2.2 Asset Managers and Asset Owners

| Entity type | AUM threshold | Applies from |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| UK-authorised asset managers (UCITS management companies, AIFMs, MiFID portfolio managers) | Assets under management **≥ £50 billion** | Financial years beginning **on or after 1 January 2022** |
| UK-authorised asset managers | AUM **≥ £5 billion** (and < £50bn) | Financial years beginning **on or after 1 January 2023** |
| Occupational pension schemes (TPR regulated) | Assets **≥ £1 billion** | From **1 October 2021** (first report by 31 October 2022) |
| Occupational pension schemes | Assets **≥ £500 million** | From **1 October 2022** |

Asset managers must publish an entity-level report on their website and produce product-level disclosures referencing how climate-related risks and opportunities are integrated into investment decisions.

### 2.3 Life Insurers and FCA-Regulated Pension Providers

Large UK life insurers and FCA-regulated pension providers are subject to TCFD-aligned disclosure requirements under COBS/SUP rules. Thresholds and timelines broadly mirror those for asset managers above based on assets under management or administration.

---

## 3. What Must Be Disclosed?

In-scope entities must produce disclosures aligned with the TCFD's four pillars and eleven recommended disclosures:

**Governance**
- The board's oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities
- Management's role in assessing and managing climate-related risks and opportunities

**Strategy**
- Climate-related risks and opportunities the organisation has identified over the short, medium, and long term
- Impact of those risks and opportunities on the organisation's business, strategy, and financial planning
- Resilience of the organisation's strategy under different climate-related scenarios, including a 2°C or lower scenario

**Risk Management**
- The organisation's processes for identifying and assessing climate-related risks
- The organisation's processes for managing climate-related risks
- How these processes are integrated into the organisation's overall risk management

**Metrics and Targets**
- Metrics used to assess climate-related risks and opportunities
- Scope 1, Scope 2, and (if appropriate) Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions and related risks
- Targets used to manage climate-related risks and opportunities and performance against targets

---

## 4. Comply-or-Explain

For listed companies, the FCA uses a **comply-or-explain** approach. Entities that cannot or do not make all recommended disclosures must explain why in their annual report. The FCA has stated it will monitor the quality and consistency of explanations and has indicated that blanket explanations citing data unavailability are insufficient over time.

Asset manager and life insurer requirements are **mandatory**, not comply-or-explain, for entities above the applicable AUM thresholds.

---

## 5. Where Must Disclosures Appear?

| Entity type | Required location |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Listed companies | Annual financial report (within the body of the annual report, not an appendix) |
| Asset managers | Publicly accessible webpage (entity-level report); product-level disclosures within fund documentation |
| Life insurers / pension providers | Publicly accessible webpage; within product-level documents where required |

---

## 6. Scenario Analysis

The TCFD framework recommends that organisations assess strategy resilience using climate scenario analysis. The FCA expects entities to disclose:

- Which scenarios were used
- Key assumptions underlying scenarios
- The time horizons considered (short: up to 3 years; medium: 3–10 years; long: 10+ years, as defined by the entity)

The FCA has not mandated the use of specific scenarios but expects consistency with established published scenarios (e.g., IEA Net Zero 2050, NGFS scenarios).

---

## 7. Interaction with UK Sustainability Disclosure Standards (UK SDS)

The UK government is developing **UK Sustainability Disclosure Standards (UK SDS)** based on the ISSB's IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 standards. Once UK SDS are mandated (expected for large UK companies from 2026 onwards, subject to consultation), they will likely supersede or significantly extend the FCA TCFD rules for listed companies.

The FCA has indicated it will consult on updating its disclosure rules to reflect UK SDS once the standards are endorsed by the UK government. Companies currently complying with FCA TCFD rules should treat their existing disclosures as a foundation for future UK SDS compliance.

---

## 8. Enforcement and Supervisory Expectations

The FCA reviews annual reports and has issued supervisory letters to listed companies citing common deficiencies, including:

- Boilerplate governance disclosures that do not reflect actual board processes
- Failure to disclose Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions with appropriate methodology explanation
- Scenario analysis that is qualitative-only without quantitative assessment of financial impact
- Targets that are aspirational without baselines or interim milestones

Persistent non-compliance or inadequate explain statements may result in FCA supervisory action.
