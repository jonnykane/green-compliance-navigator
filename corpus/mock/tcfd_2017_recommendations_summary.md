## 1. Overview

The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) was established by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) in 2015 to develop voluntary, consistent climate-related financial risk disclosures for use by companies in providing information to investors, lenders, insurers, and other stakeholders.

The TCFD published its final recommendations in June 2017. The framework is organised around **four pillars** (Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, Metrics and Targets) and contains **eleven recommended disclosures** across those pillars.

The TCFD recommendations are now the basis for mandatory disclosure regimes in the UK, including FCA rules for listed companies and asset managers (PS21/24) and the UK government's own TCFD-aligned reporting requirements for large companies.

## 2. The Four TCFD Pillars and Eleven Recommended Disclosures

### 2.1 Governance

Disclose the organisation's governance around climate-related risks and opportunities.

**Recommended disclosures:**

1. **Board oversight** — Describe the board's oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities.
2. **Management's role** — Describe management's role in assessing and managing climate-related risks and opportunities.

### 2.2 Strategy

Disclose the actual and potential impacts of climate-related risks and opportunities on the organisation's businesses, strategy, and financial planning, where such information is material.

**Recommended disclosures:**

3. **Risks and opportunities identified** — Describe the climate-related risks and opportunities the organisation has identified over the short, medium, and long term.
4. **Impact on business, strategy, and financial planning** — Describe the impact of climate-related risks and opportunities on the organisation's businesses, strategy, and financial planning.
5. **Strategy resilience** — Describe the resilience of the organisation's strategy, taking into consideration different climate-related scenarios, including a 2°C or lower scenario.

### 2.3 Risk Management

Disclose how the organisation identifies, assesses, and manages climate-related risks.

**Recommended disclosures:**

6. **Processes for identifying and assessing risks** — Describe the organisation's processes for identifying and assessing climate-related risks.
7. **Processes for managing risks** — Describe the organisation's processes for managing climate-related risks.
8. **Integration into overall risk management** — Describe how processes for identifying, assessing, and managing climate-related risks are integrated into the organisation's overall risk management.

### 2.4 Metrics and Targets

Disclose the metrics and targets used to assess and manage relevant climate-related risks and opportunities where such information is material.

**Recommended disclosures:**

9. **Metrics used** — Disclose the metrics used by the organisation to assess climate-related risks and opportunities in line with its strategy and risk management process.
10. **Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions** — Disclose Scope 1, Scope 2, and, if appropriate, Scope 3 greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, and the related risks.
11. **Targets and performance** — Describe the targets used by the organisation to manage climate-related risks and opportunities and performance against targets.

## 3. Scenario Analysis Guidance

The TCFD places particular emphasis on **scenario analysis** as a tool for understanding strategic resilience. Key guidance:

- Organisations should use **climate-related scenarios** to assess the resilience of their strategy under a range of plausible futures.
- The TCFD recommends considering scenarios consistent with **a 2°C or lower global temperature rise** (in line with the Paris Agreement), as well as scenarios consistent with higher temperature pathways (e.g., a 4°C scenario reflecting current policy trajectories).
- The **well-below 2°C scenario** (as referenced in the Paris Agreement) should be considered where feasible.
- Scenarios should cover **short-term** (typically up to 2 years), **medium-term** (2–5 years), and **long-term** (5+ years) time horizons, although the TCFD acknowledges that organisations define time horizons differently and should disclose how they define these for their business context.
- Scenario analysis need not be quantitative — qualitative analysis is acceptable, especially for organisations new to the process — but the TCFD encourages progression toward quantitative disclosure over time.

## 4. Applicability to UK Listed Companies

Following the publication of the TCFD 2017 recommendations, the UK moved to mandate TCFD-aligned disclosures for several categories of companies:

- **Premium listed companies**: required to make TCFD-aligned disclosures in annual reports for financial years beginning on or after **1 January 2021** (FCA LR 9.8.6R).
- **Standard listed companies** (equity shares): required from financial years beginning on or after **1 January 2022** (FCA LR 14.3.27R).
- **Large UK companies** (not necessarily listed): the UK government extended TCFD-aligned reporting requirements to large private companies and LLPs above certain size thresholds from April 2022, under amendments to the Companies Act 2006 and the LLP Regulations.
- **Asset managers and asset owners**: subject to FCA rules under PS21/24, with phased thresholds based on assets under management (≥£50 billion from 2022; ≥£5 billion from 2023).

UK listed companies must disclose against all eleven TCFD recommended disclosures on a **comply-or-explain** basis, explaining any omissions in their annual financial report.

## 5. Transition and Physical Risks

The TCFD distinguishes two broad categories of climate-related risk:

**Transition risks** — risks associated with the transition to a lower-carbon economy, including:
- Policy and legal risks (e.g., carbon pricing, emissions regulations)
- Technology risks (e.g., substitution of existing products/services)
- Market risks (e.g., changing customer behaviour, commodity price shifts)
- Reputational risks

**Physical risks** — risks arising from the physical impacts of climate change, including:
- **Acute** physical risks: event-driven risks such as increased severity of extreme weather events (floods, storms, heat waves)
- **Chronic** physical risks: longer-term shifts such as sustained higher temperatures, sea-level rise, and changes in precipitation patterns

Organisations are expected to identify and disclose both transition and physical risks relevant to their business and geographies.
