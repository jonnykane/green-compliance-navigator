## 1. What is ESOS?

The Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme (ESOS) is a mandatory energy assessment scheme for large UK businesses, implemented under the **Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme Regulations 2014** (SI 2014/1643). It implements the UK's obligations under Article 8 of the EU Energy Efficiency Directive, retained and amended post-Brexit.

ESOS requires qualifying organisations to carry out comprehensive audits of their energy use across all buildings, industrial processes, and transport, and to identify cost-effective energy-saving opportunities. Participation does not require organisations to implement the identified measures — the obligation is to **audit and notify**, not to act.

---

## 2. Who Must Comply?

An organisation must participate in ESOS if it is a **large undertaking** on the **qualification date** (which is the 31 December immediately preceding each compliance deadline).

An organisation is a **large undertaking** for ESOS purposes if it meets **either** of the following criteria:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Employees | 250 or more employees (full-time equivalent) |
| Turnover AND balance sheet | Annual turnover exceeding **£44 million** AND annual balance sheet total exceeding **£38 million** |

**Note:** The employee threshold is an OR condition with the financial thresholds — satisfying either employees ≥250, OR both financial thresholds, brings an organisation into scope.

### 2.1 Group Structures

For UK corporate groups, the qualification assessment applies at the **highest UK-registered parent level**. A UK subsidiary within a qualifying group is in scope regardless of its own size. Overseas parents are also assessed — a non-UK parent with UK-registered subsidiaries may qualify the UK entities for ESOS if the group as a whole meets the thresholds.

### 2.2 Exemptions

Organisations that have a valid **ISO 50001** certification covering their total energy consumption are exempt from conducting separate ESOS audits (but must still notify compliance to the Environment Agency).

Public sector bodies are **not** required to participate in ESOS (they are subject to the Greening Government Commitments framework instead), although public sector-owned trading companies may be in scope.

---

## 3. What Does ESOS Require?

Qualifying organisations must:

1. **Measure total energy consumption** across all areas: buildings, industrial processes, and transport used for business purposes
2. **Conduct energy audits** covering at least **90% of total energy consumption** (by value), identifying energy efficiency improvement opportunities
3. **Appoint a Lead Assessor** — a qualified energy auditor who oversees and signs off the assessment
4. **Notify the Environment Agency (EA)** of compliance by the compliance deadline, using the ESOS notification system

The energy audit must:
- Identify significant areas of energy consumption
- Identify cost-effective energy-saving recommendations
- Be based on current, representative, and measured data
- Be documented sufficiently for the Lead Assessor to sign off

## 4. Compliance Phases and Deadlines

ESOS operates on a **4-year compliance cycle**. Each phase has a compliance deadline by which qualifying organisations must have completed their audit and notified the EA.

| Phase | Qualification date | Compliance deadline | Status |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|-|
| Phase 1 | 31 December 2014 | **5 December 2015** | Closed |
| Phase 2 | 31 December 2018 | **5 December 2019** | Closed |
| Phase 3 | 31 December 2022 | **5 June 2024** | Closed (deadline passed) |
| Phase 4 | 31 December 2026 | **5 December 2027** | Upcoming |

**Phase 3 note:** The compliance deadline for Phase 3 was **5 June 2024**. Organisations that missed this deadline are in breach of the Regulations and may be subject to enforcement action by the EA. Late compliance does not extinguish the obligation — organisations should complete and notify as soon as possible.

---

## 5. Lead Assessors

Every ESOS assessment must be led and signed off by an **ESOS Lead Assessor**. Lead Assessors must be members of a recognised professional body scheme approved for ESOS purposes, including:

- Energy Institute (EI)
- Chartered Institution of Building Services Engineers (CIBSE)
- Association of Energy Engineers (AEE)
- Chartered Institution of Water and Environmental Management (CIWEM)
- Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET)

Organisations may use an in-house Lead Assessor (if qualified) or appoint an external consultant.

---

## 6. Administered By

ESOS is administered by the following bodies depending on jurisdiction:

| UK nation | Administering body |
|-----------|-------------------|
| England | Environment Agency (EA) |
| Scotland | Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA) |
| Wales | Natural Resources Wales (NRW) |
| Northern Ireland | Northern Ireland Environment Agency (NIEA) |

UK-wide groups must notify the EA (England) as the **primary compliance body**, with copies to relevant devolved bodies if operations span multiple nations.

---

## 7. Enforcement

The EA has powers to issue **compliance notices**, **penalty notices**, and **enforcement undertakings** for failure to comply. Civil penalties for non-compliance can be significant. The EA publishes names of non-compliant organisations.

---

## 8. Relationship to Other Energy and Carbon Reporting

ESOS sits alongside — but does not replace — other reporting obligations:

| Scheme | Frequency | Focus |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| ESOS | 4-yearly | Energy audit and opportunity identification |
| SECR | Annual | Energy use and carbon emissions reporting in annual accounts |
| TCFD / FCA rules | Annual | Climate-related financial risk disclosure |
| UK ETS | Annual | Carbon allowance trading (energy-intensive industries) |
| Climate Change Agreements (CCAs) | Ongoing | Reduced climate change levy in exchange for energy efficiency targets |

Data collected for SECR reporting (annual energy consumption figures) can be used as input to ESOS assessments, reducing duplication.
